### [CHUNK FLEX-02] Objectives, hypothesis, approach | source: S1 | tags: objectives, hypothesis, approach
- Objectives [SRC]: (1) map flammability limits of liquid fuels in microgravity; (2) measure effectiveness of gaseous suppressants (CO2, He, SF6); (3) build predictive models of combustion limits in reduced gravity, validating simplified models by adding detailed chemistry, heat transfer and transport; (4) obtain high-resolution droplet burning rate, extinction, radiation, soot and temperature data across ambient conditions and suppressant levels.
- Hypothesis [SRC]: absence of buoyant convection changes species/energy transport, radiative loss and kinetics, so flammability limits and suppressant efficacy differ from normal gravity.
- Approach [SRC]: ignite relatively large droplets and determine the Limiting Oxygen Index (LOI) as a function of ambient pressure (0.5-1.5 atm), then repeat with suppressants. Includes slow, sub-buoyant flow tests made by translating the droplet at a slow uniform speed, and many free (fiber-free) droplets with slow drift.
- Claimed Earth benefits [SRC]: fire safety in confined or reduced-gravity settings (submarines, high-altitude aircraft); less harmful suppressants; cool-flame chemistry informing engines with better efficiency and lower emissions.
- [INF] The PSI text names "He, CO2, SF6" while the 2013 slides (S3) say N2, He, CO2 and xenon were flown. See FLEX-14.

### [CHUNK FLEX-03] Core concepts and equations (LaTeX) | source: S4, S5, S7, S6 | tags: equations, theory, glossary
1. Classical d-square law (no soot, no radiation, quasi-steady) [SRC, S4]:
   $$\left(\frac{D}{D_0}\right)^2 = 1 - K_0\,\frac{t}{D_0^2}, \qquad \text{equivalently } D^2 = D_0^2 - K t$$
   Real droplets deviate. K depends on $D_0$ (S4, FLEX-09).
2. Standoff ratios [SRC, S4]: flame standoff ratio $\mathrm{FSR}=D_f/D$, soot-shell standoff ratio $\mathrm{SSR}=D_s/D$.
3. Limiting Oxygen Index and Minimum Extinction Concentration [SRC, S7]:
   $$\mathrm{LOI} = 0.209\,(1-\mathrm{MEC})$$
   LOI is the lowest O2 mole fraction that supports a quasi-steady flame. For total flooding, the agent must drop O2 below LOI. [SRC]
4. Damkohler framing [SRC S7, definition added] The flammability boundary is essentially the critical Damkohler number, $\mathrm{Da}=\tau_{\text{residence}}/\tau_{\text{chemistry}}$. Below the critical value the flame cannot be sustained. The exact definition is not given in the source. [INF]
5. Two classical extinction modes [SRC, S3, S7]: diffusive/blowoff extinction (high O2, small droplet; red branch, Law 1975) and radiative extinction (low O2, large droplet; blue branch, Chao et al. 1990). After extinction the droplet continues as pure evaporation.
6. Generic overall-reaction form used in activation-energy analyses [INF]:
   $$\dot{\omega} = B\,\exp\!\left(-\frac{E_a}{R T}\right)[\text{Fuel}]^{a}[\text{O}_2]^{b}$$
   Nayagam et al. extracted an effective heat release, overall activation energy and pre-exponential factor from burning rates and extinction diameters. The numeric values are not in the supplied text. [FLAG]
7. Methanol two-step global kinetics (Zhang 1996, in S6) [SRC]:
   $$2\,\mathrm{CH_3OH}+\mathrm{O_2}\rightarrow \mathrm{H_2O}+\mathrm{CO_2}+3\,\mathrm{H_2}+\mathrm{CO}, \qquad \mathrm{H_2}+\mathrm{CO}+\mathrm{O_2}\rightarrow \mathrm{H_2O}+\mathrm{CO_2}$$
   Reaction II is written schematically in the slide. It is not stoichiometrically scaled to the H2 produced in reaction I.
8. Methanol extinction-diameter theory (Zhang, Card, Williams 1996, via S6) [SRC]: extinction diameter $d_e$ is linear in initial diameter, $d_e \propto d_0$. Water produced in the flame diffuses back and condenses on the droplet, builds up in the second half of burning, and at a threshold begins to evaporate with the fuel. That water reaches the flame zone, lowers flame temperature, raises reaction time, and the flame extinguishes when residence time is insufficient.
9. Simulation governing equations (S5) [SRC, summarized]: 1-D spherosymmetric transient species and energy conservation in both phases; heat flux = conduction + enthalpy transport by species diffusion + Dufour effect + spectral radiation; Stefan-Maxwell diffusion; far field at 200 x initial droplet diameter, fixed composition and 298 K. Interface equilibrium (reconstructed from garbled text) [INF]: $x_{i,\text{gas}}\,p=\gamma_i\,x_{i,\text{liq}}\,p_{\text{vap},i}(T)$.

### [CHUNK FLEX-04] LOI test procedure | source: S1, S7 | tags: procedure, LOI, flammability-map
Construct the flammability map (O2 mole fraction vs droplet diameter) as follows [SRC]:
1. Select test pressure 0.5-1.5 atm.
2. Select a smaller droplet (about 3 mm).
3. Choose an O2 fraction, based on ground tests, that lets the droplet burn to completion.
4. Run a droplet combustion test.
5. If the flame extinguishes at a finite diameter, enlarge the droplet or repeat.
6. Continue until the flame survives only briefly after ignition (radiative extinction).
7. Repeat at lower O2 fraction.
8. Continue to the O2 fraction where the droplet does not ignite.
If extinction sizes are too small to measure at every O2 level, run an iterative sequence (burn to completion, then no-ignition) with finer O2 increments to bracket LOI. [SRC]
Slow-flow tests: duplicate runs that gave finite extinction diameters, then near the end impose a small sub-buoyant flow by translating the droplet. Hardware could not diagnose a uniform steady convective flow over a whole test. [SRC]
Suppressant tests repeat the same procedure at fixed suppressant level, then step the level and type. [SRC]
Conceptual map (S7 Fig. 2): flammable region inside a U-shaped curve; red branch = extinction (blowoff), blue branch = radiative quench; LOI = point where they meet (lowest O2 supporting a quasi-steady flame). [SRC]

### [CHUNK FLEX-05] Suppressant efficacy: MEC, LOI, CO2 vs He | source: S7 | tags: suppression, CO2, helium, MEC, LOI, calc
- ISS US segment uses CO2 as suppressant. [SRC]
- Cup-burner methane flames, standard air (Takahashi et al. 2004), MEC [SRC]:
  CO2: 0.16 (1-g), 0.19 (micro-g). He: 0.27 (1-g), 0.31 (micro-g). N2: 0.26 (1-g), 0.38 (micro-g).
- LOI from $\mathrm{LOI}=0.209(1-\mathrm{MEC})$ [CALC]:
  | Agent | LOI 1-g | LOI micro-g | change |
  | CO2 | 0.176 | 0.169 | -0.007 |
  | He  | 0.153 | 0.144 | -0.008 |
  | N2  | 0.155 | 0.130 | -0.025 |
  (rounded to 3 decimals)
- Reading [INF]: lower MEC means a more effective agent. By cup-burner MEC, CO2 is the most effective of the three in both gravity levels. N2 loses the most effectiveness in micro-g (MEC rises 0.26 to 0.38).
- Conflicting evidence [SRC]: Honda and Ronney 1998 (opposed-flow flame spread over thin solids with He, N2, Ar, CO2, SF6) suggest CO2 is less effective than He in microgravity on a minimum-O2 basis. The two results differ in fuel and geometry, so no single ranking holds. [INF]
- Katta et al. 2004 (methane cup burner, simulation plus micro-g experiment): CO2 above 19.1 vol% needed to destabilize the flame base in micro-g, vs 16.4 vol% in normal gravity. Gas-phase radiation is important for micro-g limits. [SRC]
- Radiative feedback: with no buoyant convection, flame radiative losses become a large share of heat loss; a thermally participating gas like CO2 absorbs and re-radiates, raising fuel-side temperatures (Chernovsky et al. 2005, ethylene flame on a spherical porous burner, 20-40% CO2). This is why CO2 efficacy in micro-g is not assured. [SRC]

### [CHUNK FLEX-07] Cool-flame discovery (n-heptane) | source: S3, S4-ref, S5 | tags: cool-flame, discovery, heptane, fire-safety
- Conventional view before FLEX [SRC, S3]: a droplet flame extinguishes by diffusive extinction (high O2, small droplet) or radiative extinction (low O2, large droplet), followed by pure evaporation. Cool flames were traditionally tied to premixed combustion and engine knock (cool flame leads to hot ignition).
- Anomaly [SRC]: after radiative extinction of a large n-heptane droplet (the visible flame disappears), the droplet keeps regressing at a quasi-steady burning rate for an extended period, ends at a finite "cool-flame extinction" diameter, and then a vapor cloud rapidly appears around the droplet. [SRC, Nayagam et al. 2012 abstract on S3 slide]
- Hypothesis: the second stage is sustained by low-temperature, soot-free "cool-flame" chemical heat release. Measured burning rates and extinction diameters give effective activation energy and pre-exponential values closer to literature cool-flame values than hot-flame values. [SRC]
- Never before seen in 50+ years of drop-tower, parabolic-flight and shuttle droplet experiments. Published after critical review by skeptical reviewers. [SRC]
- Signature (slide): low heat release rate, quasi-steady burning, a second extinction, lower activation energy.
- Conditions: n-heptane in air at 1 atm; also 18% O2 in N2 (82%) and 18/67/15 O2-N2-CO2. Later tests show similar behavior for n-octane and n-decane. [SRC]
- Fire-safety implication [SRC, authors' interpretation]: the cool flame can persist after hot-flame extinction, generating partially oxidized, combustible vapor that can re-ignite, akin to smoldering in solid fuels. Recent results show re-ignition with decane. [SRC]
- Second-stage temperature debate [SRC, S5 intro]: Nayagam's analysis allowed 1000-1400 K for the second stage if heat release were 50%, but a true cool flame at the NTC maximum would sit near 700 K with heptyl ketohydroperoxide, formaldehyde and alkenes as main products instead of H2O, CO2 and CO. Simulation later gave about 750 K (FLEX-08).
- Independent support [SRC]: Cuoci, Frassoldati, Faravelli, Ranzi (Politecnico di Milano) reported cool-flame second-stage burning in simulations of the same case (21% O2, d0 = 3.91 mm).
- Why the classical mechanism needs care [SRC, S5]: low-temperature oxidation chemistry (R + O2 to RO2 to QOOH to O2QOOH to ketohydroperoxide + OH) normally needs partial premixing, so a diffusive low-temperature flame was surprising.

### [CHUNK FLEX-08] Numerical modelling of the cool flame | source: S5, S3 | tags: simulation, kinetics, heptane, radiation, parametric
Model [SRC]: transient 1-D spherosymmetric droplet code with detailed gas-phase kinetics, spectral radiation and multicomponent transport.
Kinetics [SRC]: Curran et al. n-heptane mechanism (1038 species, 2739 reactions) reduced by path flux analysis (PFA) to 128 species and 565 reactions; reproduces shock-tube ignition delay (1, 13.5, 30 atm) and flow-reactor oxidation (8 atm) including the NTC region. A high-temperature-only mechanism (Chaos et al. 2007) is used as a control.
Base case [SRC]: d0 = 3.91 mm, 21% O2 / 79% N2, 1 atm.
- High-temperature-only model: radiative extinction at large size, then pure evaporation; extinguishes earlier than experiment.
- Detailed model: radiative extinction (first stage), then a second stage with significantly higher burning rate and a flame hugging the droplet at about 750 K, then extinction near the end. First-stage peak temperature near 2000 K. Reproduces experiment qualitatively. [SRC]
- Quantitative gap [SRC]: second-stage burning rate is over-predicted and extinction diameter under-predicted: about 0.35 mm, a factor of 3.5 below experiment (so experiment is about 1.2 mm [CALC]).
- Heat budget (S5 Fig. 4) [SRC, numbers FIG]: Q_generation spikes to about 380 W at ignition, falls to about 100 W by the end of stage 1; Q_radiation plateaus near 85 W and Q_conduction near 38 W, so radiation is about 2x conduction in stage 1. Transition to stage 2 when total losses meet generation (about 6-7 s in simulation). Stage-2 generation is about 17 W (about 11% of stage 1), and the dominant loss switches from radiation to conduction.
- Mechanism of transition [SRC]: heat loss (not radiation specifically) moves the flame close to the droplet and drops temperature into the cool-flame window.
- Parametric studies (all n-heptane, 1 atm) [SRC, details FIG]:
  - Initial diameter 2-7 mm in air: larger d0 gives an earlier transition; 2 and 3 mm stay single-stage, 4 mm and above go two-stage.
  - CO2 added to a 3.0 mm droplet: 0% CO2 single-stage; 5% to 70% CO2 triggers the cool flame, and more CO2 means earlier transition (about 0.8 s/mm^2 at 5% down to about 0.1 s/mm^2 at 30%+ [FIG]).
  - He added to a 3.0 mm droplet: also triggers the transition earlier, via enhanced conductive loss. Initial burning rate rises (up to about 0.9 mm^2/s at 70% He [FIG]), but the second stage ends sooner and a larger residual droplet remains [FIG, INF].
- Kinetic tuning (S3) [SRC]: Curran chemistry with PFA reduction; "revised" model changed two sensitive rates: QOOHO2 to QOOH + O2 (A factor x2) and QOOHO2 to ketohydroperoxide + OH (A factor /2). Revised kinetics improved ignition-delay and flow-reactor reactivity in the low-temperature region, a case where droplet data improves kinetics used by other methods. Conclusion: widely used detailed models do not accurately predict second-stage cool-flame extinction.
- Take-home [INF]: any enhanced heat loss (radiative CO2, conductive He, large size) can push a droplet into the cool-flame branch. This directly links FLEX suppressant studies to cool-flame hazard: suppression by dilution may convert a hot flame into a cool one rather than extinguishing it.

### [CHUNK FLEX-09] Initial-diameter effects: n-octane and n-decane | source: S4 | tags: droplet-size, soot, radiation, fiber, octane, decane
- Question [SRC]: how burning rate $K_0$ depends on initial diameter $D_0$ over the widest range ever done for one fuel.
- Range [SRC]: $0.52\le D_0\le 0.85$ mm in a ground 1.2 s drop tower (fibers 14 um SiC); $2.16\le D_0\le 4.97$ mm on ISS (MDCA). No data for 0.85-1.7 mm because drop-tower time is too short and ISS deployment near 1.5 mm could impart drift. Conditions: room-temperature air, 1 atm. Fuels n-octane (to 3.61 mm) and n-decane (to 4.97 mm). [SRC]
- MDCA method [SRC]: fuel pushed through hollow needles into a liquid bridge, needles retracted fast, hot-wire igniters, free-floating or 80 um SiC-tethered droplets; Hi-Beam backlit camera (1 MP, 30 fps), color camera, and low-light UV camera for OH emission. Droplet, flame and soot-shell diameters extracted by image analysis (Dembia et al. 2012 Matlab tool).
- Findings [SRC]:
  1. $K$ and FSR decrease with increasing $D_0$ in every time interval; SSR increases with $D_0$.
  2. Small droplets (0.52-0.85 mm; radiation negligible): lower $K$ with larger $D_0$, attributed to more soot and longer fuel residence time.
  3. Large droplets (2.16-4.97 mm): $K$ still decreases, but the flame is less luminous (less soot), so the cause is increasing radiative loss reducing heat feedback to the droplet.
  4. Radiative extinction visible for octane at $D_0 = 3.61$ mm (about 1.0 s/mm^2) with a clear burning-rate drop; post-extinction burning is slower than before but still faster than pure evaporation. For decane with $D_0>3.97$ mm extinction is subtler (flame disappearance); pre-extinction $K$ is lower than octane, partly attributed to higher liquid density.
  5. After radiative extinction the soot shell keeps expanding while no new soot forms; suggested cause is weaker thermophoresis once the hot flame is gone.
  6. Fiber effect: the 80 um SiC fiber induces convection around ~1-2 mm droplets (flame displaced farther, higher $K$ and FSR); negligible for $D_0\approx 4.97$ mm. New idea: fibers may act through gas motion, not only heat conduction.
- Link to cool flames [INF]: the post-extinction burning rate being above pure evaporation in octane and decane is consistent with the second-stage burning in heptane (FLEX-07). The paper does not use the term "cool flame".

### [CHUNK FLEX-10] Methanol: water absorption, evaporation, extinction | source: S6 | tags: methanol, water, extinction, OH-star, ZGF, CO2
- Why methanol [SRC]: simple kinetics, almost no soot, small flame standoff (minimal radiation effects), and combustion water condenses on the droplet, a model for bi-component fuels.
- Mixing limits [SRC]: diffusion-limited (thin water boundary layer, evaporates early with the fuel) vs convection-limited (homogeneous droplet, more water, more water in the vapor).
- ISS FLEX methanol tests [SRC]: O2 13-21% with CO2/N2 diluents, 1 atm. Plot of extinction diameter vs initial diameter forms two clusters: 13-16% O2 near the no-burn line (little burning before extinction), 18-21% O2 much smaller extinction diameters. [FIG, INF]
- Example ISS test FLEX-145 [SRC, with conflict]: slide 10: P = 763.5 mmHg, Y_O2 0.199, Y_N2 0.749, Y_CO2 0.050, $D_0$ = 3.33 mm, $D_{ext}$ = 1.613 mm, $K_{avg}$ = 0.448 mm^2/s; date 2010-11-16. Slide 9 gives conflicting composition and size (see FLEX-13).
- Ground (ZGF drop tower) tests [SRC]: 16 methanol tests, 21% O2 with Ar/N2 diluents, 0.5-2 atm. Ar/N2 mimics CO2/N2 without gas-phase radiation. $D_0$ about 1.5-2.0 mm gave $d_e$ about 0.65-1.05 mm, nearly linear [FIG]. Example H-8-04: 1 atm, 21% O2 / 35% Ar / 44% N2, $D_0$ = 1.69 mm, $D_{ext}$ = 0.79 mm.
- Method [SRC]: OH* chemiluminescence from the Low-Light-Level Ultra-Violet (LLUV) camera in the ISS runs; blue-flame pixel intensity from the 8-bit color camera in the ZGF. Flame intensity falls when water starts to evaporate (about 3 s in H-8-04); this marks the water-flux transition diameter $d_c$ (condensation to evaporation). $d_c$ (about 0.75-1.8 mm) sits above $d_e$ in the ZGF data [FIG]. Summary: flame-intensity analysis gives the water-flux transition point; a color camera may be an adequate substitute.
- Future work [SRC]: extend analysis to the full FLEX methanol data set, refine correlations, compare with theory.

### [CHUNK FLEX-12] Cross-source synthesis and insights | source: all | tags: insights, synthesis
1. Heat loss is the control variable. Radiation (large droplet, CO2) and conduction (He) both drive the transition from hot flame to cool flame (S5), and radiative loss controls burning-rate decline at large size (S4). One mechanism ties the suppressant tests (S7) to the cool-flame discovery (S3).
2. Suppression by dilution may not be complete extinction. CO2 and He at modest levels push heptane droplets into a cool-flame branch with persistent vapor generation and possible re-ignition (S3, S5). Fire-safety margins should therefore be set by the end of the cool-flame stage, not just the loss of visible flame. [INF]
3. Microgravity changes suppressant ranking. N2 is far less effective in micro-g by MEC (0.26 to 0.38), CO2 less so (0.16 to 0.19); other studies conflict (S7). FLEX was designed to resolve this with droplets at controlled pressure and O2.
4. Visible-flame extinction is not the end of reactions. Post-extinction burning in octane and decane stays above pure-evaporation rates (S4), heptane shows a second stage (S3), and kinetic models underpredict extinction size (S5). [INF]
5. Fiber artifacts matter. Support fibers can drive micro-convection (S4), so free-droplet data are the cleaner dataset for model validation; fiber data need correction. [INF]
6. Methanol is the calibration fuel, heptane the discovery fuel. Methanol shows classical radiative/diffusive extinction plus water effects (S6); heptane shows anomalous behavior (S3, S5). Combining them gives a validation ladder for kinetics.
7. Model-readiness is high. Droplet data are small, 1-D and well characterized, and kinetics were tuned with them (S3). This supports the dashboard score model=3. [INF]
8. Dashboard relevance [INF, subjective]: FLEX strongly supports Suppression (3) and Model-ready data (3). Its relevance to Detection is nil. The seed hiO2=1 is plausible: LOI and suppressant tests run mostly at 10-40% O2, not high-O2 cabins. The cool-flame re-ignition finding may justify raising Prevention above 1; this is a judgment, not data.

### [CHUNK FLEX-15] Q&A pairs (for retrieval testing / few-shot) | tags: qa
Q: What is the DOI and PSI ID of FLEX? A: DOI 10.60555/mbq8-0451, PSI-69. [SRC]
Q: What hardware did FLEX use? A: MDCA inside the CIR of the FCF on ISS. [SRC]
Q: What does LOI mean and how does it relate to MEC? A: LOI is the lowest O2 mole fraction that supports a quasi-steady flame; $\mathrm{LOI}=0.209(1-\mathrm{MEC})$. [SRC]
Q: Is CO2 a better suppressant than He in microgravity? A: By cup-burner MEC (0.19 vs 0.31) yes, CO2 is more effective; Honda and Ronney's thin-solid flame spread results suggest the opposite. It depends on fuel and geometry. [SRC/INF]
Q: How many tests were planned and for which fuels? A: 290, split evenly between n-heptane and methanol (145 each). [CALC]
Q: What was surprising about the heptane droplets? A: After radiative extinction of the visible flame, they kept burning quasi-steadily via a low-temperature cool flame until a second extinction at a finite diameter. [SRC]
Q: What temperature does the simulated cool flame have? A: About 750 K, versus about 2000 K at the start of the hot-flame stage. [SRC/FIG]
Q: Why does a larger droplet transition to the cool-flame stage earlier? A: More radiative loss; heat loss moves the flame close to the droplet and cools it into the low-temperature window. [SRC]
Q: How does the initial droplet diameter affect the burning rate? A: Burning rate decreases as $D_0$ increases across 0.5-5 mm, due to soot and residence time when small and radiative loss when large. [SRC]
Q: Why is methanol used? A: Simple kinetics, almost no soot, small flame standoff, and water back-diffusion makes it a bi-component model. [SRC]
Q: Why does the methanol flame intensity drop during burning? A: Water that condensed starts evaporating with the fuel and cools the flame; this marks the water-flux transition diameter $d_c$. [SRC]
Q: Which numbers in the sources conflict? A: See FLEX-13 (FLEX-145 composition and size, units of K, figure captions, suppressant lists).
Q: Why does cool-flame burning matter for spacecraft fire safety? A: It generates combustible partially oxidized vapor and can re-ignite after the visible flame is gone, so flame loss does not guarantee safety. [SRC/INF]

### [CHUNK FLEX-23] FLEX Research Requirements Document (2005): design, requirements, success criteria
Source: S9 (S7 is an excerpt)
Tags: requirements, success-criteria, diagnostics, ground-program, justification

- Document: "Research Requirements Document: Droplet Flame Extinguishment in Microgravity (FLEX)", 4 Nov 2005, 81 pages (LaTeX). Research team lead F.A. Williams (UC San Diego); NASA members M. Hicks (GRC) and V. Nayagam (NCSER); external members M.Y. Choi (listed at Drexel University), F.L. Dryer (Princeton), B.D. Shaw (UC Davis). Sections: 1 Introduction, 2 Fire suppression background, 3 Droplet combustion for fire-suppression research, 4 Flight experiment description, 5 Flight experiment requirements and test matrix, 6 Post-flight analysis and success criteria, 7 Ground program, 8 Management, 9 Justification for reduced gravity, Appendix A previous droplet experiments (FSDC, FSDC-2, DCE, soot and Soret ground studies). [SRC]
- Context [SRC]: ISS US segment uses CO2 extinguishers, Russian modules water-based foam. Suppressant choice criteria in the 1985 and 1990 trade studies: effectiveness, reliability, maintainability, system weight, post-fire cleanup, toxicity, collateral damage. NASA FPDS suppression objectives: (i) suppressant effectiveness in reduced gravity, (ii) large-scale agent deployment, (iii) verify suppression-system design rules. Without buoyancy, suppressant transport, diffusion, thermophoresis and radiation dominate. Crew motion or extinguisher discharge can create flows of several cm/s (up to about 50 cm/s locally in a rack, Wu and Ruff 2003), which motivates the slow-flow tests.
- Flammability theory [SRC]: S-curve of flame temperature vs Damkohler number (Fendell 1972); Linan (1974) extinction criterion `δe = τch/τr = f(1-|γ|)` with an oxidizer-mass-fraction and temperature dependence (formula garbled in extraction) [FLAG]; Table 1 lists reduced Damkohler numbers for droplet combustion (Law 1975), stagnation-point boundary layer (Wu et al. 1982) and rotating disk (Nayagam and Williams 2000), each containing `(Tf/Ta)^3 exp(-Ta/Tf)` [INF, reconstructed from garbled text].
- Buoyancy justification [SRC/CALC]: sphericity needs diffusive residence times short vs buoyant time: `τk/τb = sqrt(gD^3)/αg` and `τm/τb = sqrt(gD^3)/Dg` (both much below 1; `αg` = thermal and `Dg` = mass diffusivity of the gas). `Gr/Re² = gβΔT D/U∞²` much below 1. Worst case `ΔT ≈ 5 K`, `D ≈ 1 cm`, `U ≈ 1 cm/s` gives required `g/g0 ≈ 10^-5`, i.e. below `10^-4 m/s²` (`10^-4/9.81 = 1.0×10^-5`). Check with `β = 1/300 K^-1`: `Gr/Re² = 16 × (g/g0)`, so `10^-5` gives `1.6×10^-4`. [CALC] 1-g tests at 1 atm would need D far below 1 mm, where extinction diameters are unmeasurable; reduced pressure at 1 g (Chung and Law 1986) covers too narrow a range, and Easton (1998) found that conditions with finite extinction diameter at 1 g burn to completion in microgravity. The 10 s JAMIC drop tower used by Dietrich et al. 2005 is no longer available.
- Independent variables (Section 4.2 and Table 4) [SRC]: O2 0.10-0.40; suppressants CO2, He, SF6 at 0-0.70 (0.70 expected for the weakest agent); pressure 0.5, 1.0, 2.0, 3.0 atm; fuels n-heptane (sooting alkane) and methanol (non-sooting alcohol with bound oxygen, small standoff); D0 2-6 mm (3 and 5 mm in the matrix); slow flow 0.1-5 cm/s by translating the fiber. DCE-1 (earlier flight, He diluent, same fuels) is the baseline.
- Hardware requirements (Table 4) [SRC]: fuel research grade, dry, degassed, purity above 99.5%, 18-27 C; droplet tolerance +/-0.25 mm, reproducibility +/-5%; fiber under 100 um; hot-wire igniter at 1-5 radii from the surface (0.5-2.5 D0), 0.5 mm positioning, igniter on 1-5 s; exclusion zone cylinder 20 D0 downstream, 8 D0 upstream, 10 D0 diameter; pressure +/-0.05 atm, held within +/-10% during a test; gas tolerance +/-0.005 (O2) and +/-0.01 (others); humidity below 10% (heptane) and below 2% (methanol); settle 2 min after fill; dwell at least 10 s before ignition; fuel vapor below 0.005; CO, CO2 and products below 0.02 (each) in atmospheres without CO2.
- Diagnostics (Table 5) [SRC]: droplet backlit view FOV 3 cm, 30 um, 30 fps, DOF 3 cm, optional 2x2 binned 60-100 fps at 60 um; OH* at 310 nm +/-5 nm, FOV 5 cm, 100 um, 30 fps; color camera FOV 5 cm, 100 um, 30 fps, near-real-time downlink; radiometers 0.6-5.0 um broadband and 1.87 um water-vapor narrow band, 30 Hz, accuracy 5% of full scale, time constant below 40 ms, at least 15 cm from the droplet, FOV 65 mm; soot volume fraction 660 nm backlight (FWHM 5 nm), 50 um, at least 5 cm FOV; soot/fiber temperature two-color LCTF pyrometry; flow velocity sampling 30 Hz; pressure/temperature 10 Hz, +/-0.01 atm, +/-0.5 C; GMT sync +/-0.03 s.
- Science end products (Table 3) [SRC]: 1a Dext vs O2 and pressure; 1b LOI vs pressure; 1c transient effects; 2a-2c LOI, Dext and heat release/radiation vs suppressant; 3 validated numerical models; 4a-4e reduced sub-models (radiation, kinetics with suppressants, soot, transport with Soret diffusion, small sub-buoyant flows).
- Flight procedure (Section 5.3) is the 8-step LOI procedure in FLEX-04; the S8 table follows it with ladders (FLEX-17).
- Success criteria (Section 6.2) [SRC]. Minimal: (1) droplet diameter plus OH* or color flame video vs time for both fuels at two pressures over a range of sizes and O2 to define LOI; (2) same with slow flow near extinction for at least 5 droplets; (3) both fuels, one pressure, two O2 levels, four CO2 fractions; (4) same with one more suppressant (He or SF6); (5) slow flow for at least 10 droplets with suppressants; (6) radiometric data at two pressures, two O2, four CO2 fractions; (7) soot volume fraction, temperature and radiometer data at P = 3 atm, O2 = 0.21 and two CO2 levels (Table 12). Complete: all diagnostics for all tests, at least four pressures, three pressures x four O2 levels, at least three suppressants, slow flow for at least 10 droplets with one quarter including soot data, soot data at three pressures and four O2 levels, and soot data for two pressures, two O2 levels and four suppressant levels. Not every test point has to succeed for Complete Success if all objectives are covered. Check against S8 in FLEX-18.
- Data policy [SRC]: archive raw images, camera settings, scale factors, radiometer data, housekeeping data in an open format such as HDF; GRC investigators responsible; public within 2 years of flight completion. Raw droplet history is derived from backlit images (Choi et al. 1989; Struk et al. 1998 methods).
- Ground program (Section 7) [SRC]: 2.2 s and 5.2 s GRC drop towers (including slow-flow rigs at 0-3 cm/s and D 1-3 mm), UC Davis high-pressure rigs, Drexel soot rig, Princeton, and GRC rigs to set the final matrix; Dryer leads detailed modeling (heptane, methanol, ethanol mechanisms, soot sub-models, inert substitution studies with CO2, He, SF6; Kroenlein et al. 2004); Shaw leads fiber-supported droplet modeling (overset grids: main cylindrical grid, droplet grid, fiber grid; Marangoni flows).
- Management (Section 8) [SRC]: Williams has overall responsibility; Nayagam and Hicks run flight-hardware work; Dryer owns the numerical model.
- Internal inconsistencies of S9 are listed in flag F16 (FLEX-13).

Source: S10, S11
Tags: FLEX-2, requirements, hardware, MDCA, fuels, ethanol, decane, mixtures

- Identity [SRC]: FLEX-2 Engineering Requirements Document FLX-REQ-0001, Final, 4 Feb 2010 (CM authorization 16 Feb 2010); prepared by ZIN Technologies (J. Robbins systems engineering lead, M. O'Toole project lead, B. Finley SpaceDOC manager) and approved by NASA GRC project scientist Michael Hicks. Export controlled EAR 99 (no license required). Three hardware domains: Chamber Insert Assembly (CIA), Avionics package, Diagnostics. CIR and MDCA launched on STS-126/ULF-2 in November 2008; FLEX-2 hardware was expected on ATV2 or ULF5 in 2010 and to be integrated after FLEX and FLEX-Helium (S11).

Binary droplet arrays on a beaded fiber with 12 mm and 24 mm spacing (three crossing-fiber pairs: two at 12 mm, one at 24 mm); the central 129 mm of the fiber is usable; fiber tension 15000 psi; fiber translation gives flow 0.1-5 cm/s +/-1%; rotation retraction 2-6 degrees/s. Hot-wire Kanthal A-1 igniters, dual-loop tips for arrays; ignition 10 s after deployment; igniter on 1-5 s; igniter 1-5 D0 from the surface +/-0.5 mm; retract after ignition; no hardware within a sphere of 10 D0 radius after ignition. [SRC]

### [CHUNK FLEX-29] Agent toolbox: formulas, filters and data schema
Source: S8, S9, S11
Tags: agent, tools, formulas, schema, python

- Use these exact definitions when computing from the flight table (`FLEX_flight_tests_clean.csv`).
- LOI and MEC: `LOI = 0.209(1-MEC)`, `MEC = 1-LOI/0.209`. At 0.7 atm the flight ladders use base O2 0.30 (30/70 N2), so use `xs = 1-xO2/0.30` there. [CALC]
- Equivalent dilution: `equiv_dilution = 1 - x_O2/0.209` (column in the clean CSV).
- Partial pressure: `pi = xi P` (atm), with P = `pressure_mmHg/760`.
- Extinction fraction: `Dext_over_D0 = Dext/D0`; near-limit if Extinction and `Dext_over_D0` at least 0.9.
- Average burning rate from table: `K_est = (D0^2-Dext^2)/t_burn` (matches the tabulated K within 15% in 87% of tests, FLEX-17).
- d-square law: `D^2 = D0^2-Kt`; droplet lifetime `τ = D0^2/K`.
- Droplet volume: `V = πD^3/6` (mm^3; 1 mL = 1000 mm^3).
- Image pixel pitch: FOV/pixels; frame storage = pixels x bits/8 x fps x seconds.
- Gas balance: `x_N2 = 1-x_O2-x_CO2-x_He` (sums to 1 within 0.015 except tests 69, 75, 76, 79, 80, 81).
- Schema of `FLEX_flight_tests_clean.csv` (274 rows, 24 columns): `test_no`, `flex_id`, `date`, `gmt`, `fuel`, `pressure_mmHg`, `pressure_atm`, `pressure_band`, `x_O2`, `x_N2`, `x_CO2`, `x_He`, `diluent`, `suppressant_fraction`, `equiv_dilution_1_minus_xO2_over_0.209`, `D0_mm`, `Dext_mm`, `Dext_over_D0`, `burning_rate_mm2_s`, `burn_time_s`, `burn_time_approx`, `test_end`, `id_group`, `flags`.
- Flags values: disruption_no_extinction_data 61, Dext_missing 49, Dext_corrupt_byte 28, K_missing 16, Dext_zero_no_visible_extinction 8, D0_missing 6, D0_corrupt_byte 6, composition_sum_off 6, duplicate_id 2, burn_time_approx 2, pressure_zero_invalid 1, Dext_gt_D0 1, K_implausibly_low 1. [CALC]
- Usable subsets: 159 Extinction tests with Dext > 0 (for extinction-diameter analysis); 262 tests with D0; 258 with K. Always drop `pressure_zero_invalid` (test 114) and treat Disruption rows as outcome-only.
- Agent behaviors recommended [INF]: (1) answer numeric questions by filtering the CSV, not from memory; (2) when asked for LOI say that no no-ignition test exists in the table and give the near-limit bracket from FLEX-19; (3) always state n for any ladder row; (4) when a question mixes FLEX and FLEX-2, check FLEX-24/26 first.
- Example filters (pandas):
  - Heptane, 1 atm, CO2: `df[(df.fuel=="heptane")&(df.pressure_band=="1.0atm")&(df.diluent=="CO2")]`
  - Near-limit set: `df[(df.test_end=="extinction")&(df.Dext_over_D0>=0.9)]`

### [CHUNK FLEX-31] Cross-source insights, version 2
Source: all
Tags: insights, synthesis, v2

1. The flight ladders make LOI a direct dilution test. At 1 atm the CO2 and He ladders hold O2/N2 at air ratio and add suppressant, so the suppressant fraction equals MEC in `LOI = 0.209(1-MEC)`. The experiment therefore measures MEC for each fuel and agent directly. [CALC]
2. Droplet and cup-burner limits agree for heptane, not for methanol. Heptane near-limit CO2 0.19-0.20 and N2 0.38 equal the microgravity cup-burner MEC (0.19, 0.38). Methanol needs CO2 about 0.33-0.36 and He about 0.38 (N2 not reached above 0.43). Agent design margins depend on fuel; an oxygenated fuel is harder to suppress with CO2. [CALC/INF]
3. CO2 lowers and He raises burning rate at fixed O2 (`K He > CO2`, p < 0.01 in both fuels), so a suppressant changes heat release, not only the limit; this also fits the simulation of S5 where He raises initial K and brings the cool-flame stage earlier. [CALC/INF]
4. Yield: 186 of 274 tests (68%) ended in Extinction and 159 (58%) have a measured Dext. Disruption (22%) removes most high-O2 and 0.7 atm high-O2 tests (15 of 17 at O2 0.30-0.34). Plan any new extinction study at O2 below 0.22. [CALC]
5. The table mixes extinction stages. In heptane in air the shortest runs end near 0.8 D0 (hot-flame radiative extinction), while a 17.5 s run ends at 1.37 mm, close to the second-stage size implied by S5. Any model-validation use of Dext must split first-stage from second-stage extinction. [INF]
6. Heat loss is still the control variable: lower pressure shifts the near-limit O2 fraction up (0.7 atm vs 1 atm: lowest O2 with a flame 0.15 vs 0.14 methanol, 0.17 vs 0.17 heptane; near-limit pO2 about 0.12-0.14 vs 0.15-0.18 atm) while partial pressure of CO2 at the methanol limit is the same (0.32 atm). A partial-pressure framing might unify limits across pressures for methanol; not for heptane. [INF, weak]
7. FLEX is a suppression experiment; FLEX-2 is a combustion-chemistry experiment. Dropped CO2/SF6, added fuel mixtures, droplet arrays, flow velocities 0.1-0.5 cm/s and OH/soot diagnostics. The cool-flame discovery in FLEX likely shaped the FLEX-2 emphasis (web articles). [INF]
8. Highest-value open items for fire safety: (a) the 2-3 atm radiative corner has only 9 tests at one condition (CO2 0.70); (b) SF6 and xenon are absent; (c) flow-velocity identification is missing from S8; (d) no "no ignition" bracket for LOI; (e) He heptane ladder stops at O2 0.16. [CALC]
9. Dashboard scoring (S2 row FLEX: [prev 1, det 0, sup 3, hiO2 1, hab 2, model 3]) [INF, subjective]: S8 supports model = 3 (274 tests with D0, Dext, K, burn time) and sup = 3 (CO2/He/N2 limits). hiO2 = 1 stays reasonable (flown O2 at most 0.34, and tests above O2 0.26 mostly end by disruption). The FLEX-2 row [1, 0, 3, 1, 2, 3] copies FLEX; S10 suggests sup should be lower (2) because CO2 is absent and the focus is fuels; this is a judgment, not data.
10. Verified cross-checks: S6 slide 10 test 145 equals S8 row 145 exactly; press-quoted 12-13% O2 limits equal S8 minima; session sizes in the press match S8 test-day counts; storage arithmetic of the CIR cameras is self-consistent (about 51 GB). [CALC]
