# T3 questions for the team's shared test set (extra-work item 3)

Five questions the T3 data can answer, two it cannot. Each answer names the finding that supports it.

## Answerable

1. Q: Does smoke from overheated spacecraft materials change as it ages in microgravity?
   A: Yes. In 42 of 44 paired SAME and SAME-R flight tests the number-mean particle diameter grew after 4 to 33 minutes of aging, by a median factor of 1.46 (F-T3-1).
2. Q: Which flown material gave the smallest smoke particles?
   A: Kapton, about 57 nm at its baseline (4 tests); Pyrell and Silicone gave the largest, about 207 to 244 nm. Each material was heated to its own baseline temperature (F-T3-3).
3. Q: Does weaker ventilation change the smoke?
   A: For Kapton, Lampwick and Silicone, runs with 0 to 2 cm/s air flow gave larger particles than runs at 8 cm/s at the same heater setting; Pyrell and Teflon did not. Groups are small (1 to 4 tests) (F-T3-2).
4. Q: Can the 2007 SAME and the 2010 SAME-R results be combined?
   A: Not without a correction: in all 5 matching comparisons SAME gave larger diameters, and SAME-R changed one instrument (F-T3-5).
5. Q: Did the modified P-Trak particle counter work in microgravity?
   A: NASA's DAFT post-flight report says it was functioning normally; it cannot see particles above 2.5 um and saturates at 500,000 particles/cm3 (F-T3-14, F-T3-22).

## Not answerable (correct response: not in our data)

6. Q: Would a spacecraft smoke detector alarm in time for a fire in a Moon habitat at 30 % oxygen and 10.2 psi?
   A: Not in our data. SAME, SAME-R and DAFT ran in ISS cabin air: no oxygen or pressure changes, no partial gravity, and no alarm thresholds in these tables. The detector timings we have reflect how the hardware moved smoke (F-T3-16).
7. Q: Does smoke from a flaming fire behave the same way?
   A: Not in our data. These tests made smoke by overheating material samples, not by flaming fires (PSI-102 page, Approach).
