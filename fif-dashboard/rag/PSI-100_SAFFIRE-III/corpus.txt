=== SAFFIRE-98-99-00 | How to read and index this corpus ===
Investigation: PSI-98, PSI-99, PSI-100
Topic: How to read and index this corpus ===
Sources: Source registry and method pointers in this corpus

Each section between === markers is a retrieval unit. Keep its ID, title, and source labels with its text. Link sample IDs to the mission ID because all three missions contain a “Sample 1”. Keep each source category with the claim it supports.

[DOC] is a value or outcome reported in a source document or the experimental table.
[CALC] is a statistic calculated from stored workbook values.
[FIG] is a qualitative observation from a workbook plot.
[INTERP] is an interpretation of the combined evidence.
[QA] is a discrepancy, coverage limit, or unresolved data issue.

Unless otherwise specified, times for sensor peaks and igniter operation are elapsed seconds from the beginning of the sample file, not seconds since ignition. A documented burn duration is the experimental table's outcome. An observed igniter window is calculated independently using current >0.5 A, from the first to the last sample in the pulse. A reported post-igniter temperature maximum is a sensor-channel maximum after the electrical pulse within the recording. Different probes measure different locations. Temperature maxima should retain their channel IDs and should not be used as direct measurements of flame temperature. Percent gas values are reported on the source scale. A change from 0.432% to 0.510% is 0.078 percentage points.

Main result [DOC, CALC]: PSI-98 compared two large SIBAL runs at a nominal 20 cm/s. The concurrent run burned for 420 s, compared with 70 s for the opposed run. PSI-99 tested nine small samples and recorded negligible low-gravity burned length in four silicone runs, full 29 cm burns in samples 5 and 6, consumption only of the PMMA portion in sample 7, and long burns with localized damage or regression in samples 8 and 9. The conditions differ across experiments, so raw burn duration alone cannot rank material flammability across missions.

=== SAFFIRE-98-99-METHOD | Uniform sensor analysis across 13 runs ===
Investigation: PSI-98, PSI-99, PSI-100
Topic: Uniform sensor analysis across 13 runs ===
Sources: [98-S1], [98-S2], [99-SENS]

Timing glossary: Igniter span is first-to-last sample above 0.5 A. Reported burn duration comes from a cited experiment document. Sustained response duration counts qualifying one-second sensor bins and can include gaps. Peak elapsed time is measured from recording start.

[CALC] Use Calibrated Data and finite numeric elapsed times. Identify the observed current pulse at >0.5 A. Baseline is the median of all channel observations before pulse onset. Post-pulse maximum is the highest individual stored measurement after the last current sample above threshold. Peak time is elapsed seconds from recording start. Delta is peak minus baseline. Time since the pulse ended is peak time minus the stated pulse end.
[CALC] To screen for sustained positive deviations, divide elapsed time into one-second bins from recording start. Evaluate full post-pulse bins with 20 or more observations. A bin qualifies if its median exceeds baseline by the greater of six unscaled baseline median absolute deviations (MAD) and a channel-specific floor. The floors are 1 °C for plume/sample thermocouples, 1 mW/cm² for radiometers, 1 cm/s for anemometers, and 0.005 in the stored units for the remaining channels, including the inlet probe. The first sustained bin is the first bin in a run of at least two consecutive qualifying bins. Total seconds is the number of qualifying bins in all such runs. The metric measures cumulative detected positive response, which can have gaps; it does not estimate burn duration. The detector does not quantify downward excursions. A dash means no qualifying sustained rise.
[QA] Thresholds are operational comparisons, not instrument specifications or significance tests. Baseline drift and fan state can influence late peaks. One-second medians suppress isolated excursions for the sustained metric; raw sample maxima remain sensitive to spikes. Channel units are the workbook units. Radiometer results here use original channels, so 98 values can differ from processed channels reported earlier.
Sources: [98-S1], [98-S2], [99-SENS], Calibrated Data.

Sources: [98-TABLE], [98-S1], [98-S2], [99-TABLE], [99-DOC], [100-TABLE], [100-S1], [100-S2], [NASA-III]

[DOC, CALC] Saffire-I and III both record large SIBAL at different nominal airflows. The sample workbooks and flight presentation support roughly 20 cm/s for Saffire-I and 25 cm/s for Saffire-III, while the PSI-100 table/Readme state 30 cm/s for III. Both concurrent runs show strongly staggered thermal peaks and large radiometer rises. The shorter Saffire-III published burn is consistent with faster propagation in that run, while the timed stop and different duration definitions limit inference from a duration ratio.
[DOC, INTERP] PSI-99 S5 and S6 also use SIBAL, at substantially narrower width. Compare width, flow direction, air speed, and diagnostic positions before transferring a spread relationship. Keep local probe temperatures, radiation, visible flame length and fuel consumption as separate observables.

=== SAFFIRE-100-TM | Telemetry coverage and clock checks ===
Investigation: PSI-100
Topic: Telemetry coverage and clock checks ===
Sources: [100-TM], [100-TM1], [100-CSV], [100-EU], [100-S1], [100-S2]
