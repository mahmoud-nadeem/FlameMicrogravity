SECTION 1 of 3.  PSI-106  SLICE  (Structure and Liftoff In Combustion Experiment)
==================================================================================================

Data analysed
-------------
  - 463 airflow velocity measurements from SLICE_BoundaryCondition.xlsx: 2009 X scan 85, 2009 Y
    scan 128, 2012 X scan 150, 2012 Y scan 75, 2012 Z scan 25 (3 of them taken with the fan
    closed).
  - Scans are at 1 mm and 4 mm above the nozzle (X and Y directions) and a vertical (Z) profile at
    (X, Y) = (9, 0) mm; the nozzle centre is the origin. Units are assumed to be cm/s and mm (the
    sheet does not state them).
  - 12 SLICE test days from the experiment table, 2012-02-09 to 2012-03-23.

106-F1.  Directly above the nozzle the air is nearly stagnant, and it recovers with height.
    What: The velocity at the nozzle centre (x = 0) is a small fraction of the largest velocity in
    the same scan, for every fan reading. At 1 mm above the nozzle it is close to zero; at 4 mm it
    is about a fifth to a third of the peak.
    Numbers:
    year  scan  height  fan readings  centre/peak min  centre/peak max
    ----  ----  ------  ------------  ---------------  ---------------
    2009  X     4 mm    7             19%              23%            
    2009  Y     4 mm    7             27%              29%            
    2012  X     1 mm    5             -1%              5%             
    2012  Y     1 mm    5             -5%              9%             
    How computed: For each scan and fan reading: velocity at position 0 divided by the maximum
    velocity of that scan.
    Caveat: Small negative values at 1 mm are probe noise around zero (the fan-closed baseline is
    -0.85 to 0.77 cm/s). A flame model that assumes a flat coflow would overstate the air reaching
    the flame base.

106-F2.  The airflow around the nozzle is not symmetric: the +X side is faster than the -X side.
    What: Close to the nozzle, velocity at +2 mm is 2.1 to 2.3 times the velocity at -2 mm (1 mm
    above, 2012 data), and at +/-3 mm it is 1.3 to 1.8 times (4 mm above, 2009 data). By about 7
    mm from the centre the two sides agree again.

    ----------  ----------  -----------  ---------  ---------  -----
    2012, 1 mm  +2 / -2 mm  55           14.4       6.2        2.33 
    2012, 1 mm  +2 / -2 mm  80           37.0       17.5       2.11 
    2012, 1 mm  +2 / -2 mm  98           48.9       23.3       2.10 
    2012, 1 mm  +2 / -2 mm  120          63.1       30.6       2.07 
    2012, 1 mm  +2 / -2 mm  139          74.9       36.4       2.06 
    2009, 4 mm  +3 / -3 mm  56           11.6       9.2        1.26 
    2009, 4 mm  +3 / -3 mm  65           18.4       12.6       1.46 
    2009, 4 mm  +3 / -3 mm  82           33.1       21.5       1.54 
    2009, 4 mm  +3 / -3 mm  98           48.2       28.5       1.69 
    2009, 4 mm  +3 / -3 mm  115          63.1       35.7       1.77 
    2009, 4 mm  +3 / -3 mm  131          73.4       42.2       1.74

    How computed: Velocities at mirrored positions from the X scans.
    Caveat: The sheet does not say why. It could be the nozzle or housing geometry, probe
    orientation or the flow entering the duct; the 'sensor flip' scan folder in PSI-106 may help.
    Treat the profile as measured, not as axisymmetric.

106-F3.  The peak velocity is set by the fan reading and does not depend on scan direction, height
        or campaign.
    What: At the two fan readings measured in both campaigns, the five scan sets agree on the peak
    velocity within 7% (reading 98) and 5% (reading 139), so the sheet's statement that the 2009
    and 2012 measurements agree well holds for the peaks. The peak rises linearly with the fan
    reading: 0.79 cm/s per reading unit (R squared 0.990, 2009 X scan at 4 mm).
    Numbers:
    fan reading  peak velocity by scan set (cm/s)                                                          spread
    -----------  ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------  ------
    98           2009 X 4mm: 56.8, 2009 Y 4mm: 53.6, 2009 Y 1mm: 53.1, 2012 X 1mm: 53.9, 2012 Y 1mm: 53.2  7%    
    139          2009 X 4mm: 81.7, 2009 Y 4mm: 80.9, 2009 Y 1mm: 78.2, 2012 X 1mm: 81.8, 2012 Y 1mm: 77.9  5%    
    How computed: Maximum velocity of each scan at the matching fan reading; straight-line fit of
    peak against fan reading.
    Caveat: Peaks only; the centre values differ between sets because they were taken at different
    heights.

106-F4.  At x = 9 mm the flow is uniform with height from 0 to 71 mm.
    What: Velocity changes by at most 12% between heights of 0 and 71 mm at every fan reading, so
    the strong structure is in the first few millimetres above the nozzle and not further up.
    Numbers:
    fan reading  velocity range (cm/s)  spread / mean
    -----------  ---------------------  -------------
    55           15.4 to 17.4           12.3%        
    80           32.6 to 35.9           9.6%         
    98           45.0 to 46.0           2.3%         
    120          56.4 to 58.1           2.9%         
    139          65.9 to 67.7           2.6%         
    How computed: Vertical profile at (X, Y) = (9, 0) mm, five heights per fan reading (2012).
    Caveat: Only one horizontal position and five heights; the profile above the nozzle centre was
    not measured here.

Limits and open questions (PSI-106)
-----------------------------------
  - No flame result (flame length, liftoff height, soot temperature) is in the files analysed;
    those come from camera images that were not used.
  - Units are assumed (cm/s, mm). Ratios in findings 1 to 3 do not depend on the unit.
  - Positions were not sampled evenly, so the 'width' of the wake can only be read to about 1 mm.
  - One nozzle size and one probe orientation; the cause of the +X/-X asymmetry is not
    established.
  - Open: where u2 was measured, and whether the 'sensor flip' and 'RandomPoints' scans explain
    the asymmetry.

SECTION 2 of 3.  PSI-107  SPICE  (Smoke Point In Coflow Experiment)
==================================================================================================

Data analysed
-------------
  - The PSI-107 experiment table: 469 rows, 34 exact duplicates removed, 435 records, grouped into
    147 flames (one ignition on one GMT day) over 11 test days (day 45 to 174).
  - The table lists conditions only (fuel, nozzle, fuel rate, coflow air velocity, ignition and
    flame-off times). It has no smoke-point or flame-length result, so everything below describes
    what was run, not where smoke appeared.

107-F1.  Fuel and nozzle are well crossed, except for methane.
    What: 17 of 18 fuel-nozzle combinations were burned. The missing one is Methane on the 1.6002
    mm nozzle. Methane has only 4 flames.
    Numbers:
    fuel           0.4064 mm  0.762 mm  1.6002 mm  total
    -------------  ---------  --------  ---------  -----
    50% Propylene  8          6         11         25   
    75% Propylene  15         8         14         37   
    Ethylene       6          8         9          23   
    Methane        2          2         0          4    
    Propane        6          4         10         20   
    Propylene      16         10        12         38   
    all            53         38        56         147  
    How computed: Count of flames by fuel and nozzle diameter.
    Caveat: Propylene appears at three strengths (100%, 75%, 50%); the balance gas of the blends
    is not stated.

107-F2.  Fuel is tied to test day, which matters for validating any model.
    What: 7 of 11 test days burned a single fuel, and methane was burned only on day 168. Holding
    out a whole test day for validation therefore also holds out whole fuels (or nearly), so day-
    grouped validation will test extrapolation to a new fuel, not just a new run.
    Numbers:
    GMT day  flames  fuels                           
    -------  ------  --------------------------------
    45       14      Ethylene                        
    48       14      Ethylene, Propane               
    52       29      75% Propylene, Propane          
    55       12      50% Propylene                   
    58       21      Propylene                       
    119      2       75% Propylene                   
    136      8       75% Propylene                   
    167      11      50% Propylene                   
    168      9       50% Propylene, Ethylene, Methane
    173      9       75% Propylene                   
    174      18      75% Propylene, Propylene        
    How computed: Fuels observed on each GMT day.
    Caveat: This is a feature of how the campaign was run. Validate both by day and by random
    flame, and say which.

107-F3.  In 99 of 147 flames (67%) the fuel rate was changed during the burn.
    What: Flames have up to 12 records. Among the 99 flames with a change, the last recorded fuel
    rate is the highest in 46 and the lowest in 39 (14 are neither), so the changes do not run in
    one direction. The earlier idea that fuel was simply stepped up until smoke appeared is not
    supported by this table alone.
    Numbers:
    fuel           flames with a fuel-rate change  flames  share
    -------------  ------------------------------  ------  -----
    50% Propylene  20                              25      80%  
    75% Propylene  29                              37      78%  
    Ethylene       7                               23      30%  
    Methane        0                               4       0%   
    Propane        16                              20      80%  
    Propylene      27                              38      71%  
    all            99                              147     67%  
    How computed: Minimum, maximum and last HC fuel rate per flame, from the experiment table.
    Caveat: A flame is defined by ignition time, so 12 flames without a usable ignition time are
    single records. Matching a result to a flame needs the smoke-point files and the time of each
    step.

107-F4.  Coflow air velocity spans 0.7 to 67.4 cm/s with 49 distinct settings.
    What: The median is 29.8 cm/s. All three nozzles cover a similar range, so coflow velocity and
    nozzle size are not confounded. 12 flames have no usable air velocity (blank, zero or negative
    entries).
    Numbers:
    nozzle     flames with air velocity  min  median  max (cm/s)
    ---------  ------------------------  ---  ------  ----------
    0.4064 mm  45                        0.7  27.9    67.4      
    0.762 mm   37                        7.2  27.9    61.8      
    1.6002 mm  53                        9.1  36.4    66.5      
    all        135                       0.7  29.8    67.4      
    How computed: Air velocity per flame (first recorded value).
    Caveat: Air velocity changes within a flame for some flames (see the 'n_values' column in
    spice_flames.csv).

107-F5.  Higher coflow velocity went with higher fuel rates; nozzle size showed no clear effect.
    What: Across 134 flames, the highest HC fuel rate used in a flame rises with coflow velocity
    (Spearman correlation 0.35, p < 0.001). In a log-log fit with fuel type as a factor (R squared
    0.54) the exponent on coflow velocity is 0.35 +/- 0.13 (95%), while nozzle diameter is -0.15
    +/- 0.15. Within ethylene alone the correlation with nozzle diameter is -0.59 (n = 22), a
    small sample.
    Numbers:
    term             exponent  95% interval  reading                      
    ---------------  --------  ------------  -----------------------------
    coflow velocity  0.35      +/- 0.13      clearly positive             
    nozzle diameter  -0.15     +/- 0.15      not distinguishable from zero
    How computed: Ordinary least squares on ln(max HC fuel rate) with ln(nozzle), ln(coflow
    velocity) and fuel dummies; flames with a fuel rate above zero and a known air velocity.
    Caveat: This describes the fuel rates that were run. It is not the smoke point: those rates
    may be where smoke appeared, or only what the operators set at each coflow. Treat it as
    exploratory until the results are joined.

107-F6.  Typical burn time is 87 s; ethylene flames burn shortest.
    What: 129 flames have a burn time (3.56 hours in total, longest 655 s). Ethylene flames have a
    median of 36 s against 72 s for propylene.
    Numbers:
    fuel           flames  median burn (s)  max burn (s)
    -------------  ------  ---------------  ------------
    Ethylene       22      36               655         
    Propylene      32      72               208         
    Propane        18      90               143         
    50% Propylene  22      100              299         
    Methane        4       102              133         
    75% Propylene  31      120              323         
    How computed: Flame-off time minus ignition time. Flames with a missing time are left out.
    Caveat: 18 flames have no burn time. The table does not say why ethylene flames were shorter.

107-F7.  12 conditions were run more than once; most repeat almost exactly.
    What: For a condition repeated on the same day (same fuel, nozzle and coflow), the median
    spread in maximum fuel rate between repeats is 1%. 2 of 12 repeated conditions differ by more
    than 30% (61%, 64%).
    How computed: Spread = (max - min) / mean of the highest fuel rate across repeated flames.
    Caveat: Some repeats may be the same setting re-run after a problem; the table does not say.
    Useful as a first estimate of run-to-run noise.

Data quality (PSI-107)
----------------------
  - 34 exact duplicate rows removed; 8 records with zero or negative air velocity set to missing;
    28 records have no usable air velocity in total.
  - 12 flames have no usable ignition time (entries such as LOS, loss of signal), kept as separate
    flames.
  - 8 flames include a fuel rate of zero.

Limits and open questions (PSI-107)
-----------------------------------
  - No smoke-point or flame-length result is attached. The Day 045 files
    (Spice_Flight_Flamelength.xls, SmokePointReview-0g-3.xlsx) are the next place to look; the
    script has attach_results() to join them.
  - Findings 3 and 5 should be re-checked once results are joined.
  - O2, pressure and the balance gas of the propylene blends are not recorded in this table.
  - Next analysis: compare with published smoke-point scaling (the University of Maryland SPICE
    thesis is a starting point) once flame lengths are available.

SECTION 3 of 3.  PSI-21  ACME CLD  (Coflow Laminar Diffusion Flame)
==================================================================================================

Data analysed
-------------
  - 385 test points that have both a data file (125 readings per second, 36 channels) and a set
    point file, from 33 test days between 2017-11-15 and 2021-03-04.
  - By year: 2017: 33 test points on 4 days, 2018: 300 test points on 25 days, 2021: 52 test
    points on 4 days (no test points in this set from the years between).
  - Flame on and off are detected from the centre radiometer; flows and set points are in volts.

21-F1.  A flame was detected in 360 of 385 test points; almost every miss had no fuel commanded.
    What: Of the 25 test points without a detected flame, 24 had a fuel set point of 0 V and 24
    are from test day 18008, which looks like flow-controller checking (names such as
    18008MFCA2_5). The exception is 18008B1: fuel was commanded for 134 s but measured fuel flow
    was on for only 7.8 s. Among test points with fuel commanded, 99.7% produced a detectable
    flame, which supports the radiometer detection rule.
    Numbers:
    group                     test points  flame detected  share 
    ------------------------  -----------  --------------  ------
    fuel set point above 0 V  351          350             99.7% 
    fuel set point 0 V        25           1               4.0%  
    no set point values       9            9               100.0%
    all                       385          360             93.5% 
    How computed: Maximum commanded fuel set point (volts) against the radiometer-based flame
    flag.
    Caveat: The detection threshold is our choice (peak rise above 0.02 V). Raw counts also differ
    from the experiment table's 'flames ignited' (344), which is counted differently.

21-F2.  The fuel controller's gas code lines up with the test type, which is a clue for decoding
        it.
    What: Code 4 and code 6 occur only on ethylene-based days; code 0 and code 2 on methane-based
    days; code 5 only on improved-coflow days (which also use code 2). This pattern is consistent
    with the codes naming the fuel gas, but it is a pattern, not a decoding.
    Numbers:
    fuel gas code  not in experiment table
    -------------  -----------------------
    0              32                     
    2              165                    
    4              88                     
    5              25                     
    6              75                     
    How computed: Test points by fuel controller (MFCA3) gas code and test type from the
    experiment table.
    Caveat: The gas code table is not in the files reviewed. Confirm before converting volts to
    physical flow.

21-F3.  More fuel flow gives a stronger radiometer signal, and ethylene flames radiate more than
        methane flames.
    What: Across 350 flames the correlation between approximate fuel flow and peak radiometer rise
    is 0.48.
    Numbers:
    test type                flames  median rise (V)  Spearman rho  log-log slope
    -----------------------  ------  ---------------  ------------  -------------
    not in experiment table  350     0.36             0.48          0.93         
    How computed: Spearman correlation and a straight-line fit on logs of peak centre-radiometer
    rise against approximate fuel flow (flames with a detected flame and fuel flow above zero).
    Caveat: Fuel concentration, burner conditions and nitrogen flow also changed between test
    days, so this is a tendency across the campaign, not a controlled comparison. Flow in slpm is
    approximate (0 to 5 V assumed to span the full scale, no gas correction).

21-F4.  Flames start about 34 s into the record and last a median of 15.2 s.
    What: Flame start is 31.9 to 36.7 s (middle half) and duration 12.6 to 30.3 s, with a range of
    0.6 to 106.2 s. The flame lasts a median of 60% of the time fuel flow is on, and duration
    follows the commanded fuel time (Spearman 0.56), so how long a flame burns is set mostly by
    how long fuel was commanded.
    How computed: Flame on and off times are where the radiometer rise exceeds 25% of its peak.
    Caveat: Measured on 125 Hz records of 21 to 244 s; the rule is ours and should be checked
    against video.

21-F5.  Thermocouples responded in 1 of 385 test points; the electric field was off in all of
        them.
    What: The one test point where a thermocouple moved is 18214K2 (range 1.71 V); in the rest the
    maximum range is 0.0005 V. Ion current has a median range of 0.029 V (maximum 7.05 V). The
    header field EField is 0 for every test point, yet the polarity code takes the values 0 (52
    test points), 1 (170 test points), 2 (163 test points).
    How computed: Range (max minus min) of each channel over the record.
    Caveat: Use the radiometer and flow channels for analysis; thermocouples are not informative
    in this set. The meaning of the polarity code is not documented in the files reviewed.

21-F6.  Test points are unevenly spread over days; most days have 16 test points.
    What: Test day 18008 has 33 test points, and 6 of 33 days have exactly 16. By test type: not
    in experiment table 385 test points.
    Numbers:
    test day  type                     test points  flames detected

    18199     not in experiment table  16           16

    Caveat: Validate any model by test day to avoid leakage between near-identical test points on

Limits and open questions (PSI-21)
----------------------------------
  - Flows are in volts, converted only approximately; the gas code table and the controller scales
    are needed.
  - Fuel concentration is known per test day only, so flame response cannot be tied to
    concentration per test point.
  - Flame detection uses our radiometer threshold; compare a few records with video before relying
    on it.
  - Open: what the polarity code means, and why the electric field is recorded as off in every
    test point.
