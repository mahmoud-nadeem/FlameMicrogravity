FLEX (Flame Extinguishment Experiment, PSI-69) - ANALYSIS FINDINGS
=====================================================================
Source: CompleteTamerRAGChunks_plus_FLEX_analysis (analysis pack v2, chunks FLEXA-01 to FLEXA-29)
Dataset: 274 ISS flight tests, 2009-03-05 to 2011-12-08 (157 methanol, 117 n-heptane)
Hardware: Multi-user Droplet Combustion Apparatus (MDCA) in the Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR)

Evidence labels used below:
  [CALC] computed from the flight table   [SRC] stated in a source document
  [INF]  interpretation / inference       [FLAG] caveat or unresolved issue
Units: burning rate K in mm^2/s, diameters in mm, pressure in atm.
Note: limits derived from the table are upper bounds on LOI, not measured LOI values.


=====================================================================
FINDING 1 - What was actually flown
=====================================================================
- 274 tests in the table vs 290 in the planned envelope; the two lists are not the same set. [CALC]
- Pressures flown: ~0.7 atm (103 tests), ~1.0 atm (161), 2-3 atm (9). No 0.5 or 1.5 atm tests. [CALC]
- O2 range flown: 0.12-0.34 (plan was 0.10-0.40). [CALC]
- Suppressants flown: CO2 and He only, plus N2-only baselines. No SF6 or xenon. [CALC]
- Diluents: N2-only 101 tests, CO2/N2 123, He/N2 50. [CALC]
- Use the flight-table rows as the record of what was flown; planning documents describe intent only.


=====================================================================
FINDING 2 - How the tests ended
=====================================================================
- Overall: 186 extinction (68%), 61 disruption (22%), 27 completion (10%). [CALC]
- Methanol: 126 extinction / 23 disruption / 8 completion.
- Heptane: 60 extinction / 38 disruption / 19 completion.
- "Extinction" = visible flame went out at a finite diameter; "Completion" = droplet burned out;
  "Disruption" = test ended by droplet disruption, so no extinction diameter. [INF]


=====================================================================
FINDING 3 - High oxygen gives a poor flammability map (disruption problem)
=====================================================================
Share of tests ending in disruption rises sharply with O2: [CALC]
  O2 <= 0.15       0 of 48 tests
  O2 0.15-0.18     2 of 67
  O2 0.18-0.22    20 of 91
  O2 > 0.22       39 of 68 (57%)
- At 0.7 atm in N2 with O2 = 0.30 or 0.34: 15 of 17 tests disrupted, 2 completed, none gave an extinction diameter.
- At 1 atm in air: 8 of 17 heptane tests were disrupted.
- Consequence: extinction-diameter data are scarce on the high-O2 side (where diffusive/blowoff extinction would show).
  Usable extinction data concentrate at O2 <= 0.21. [INF]
- The cause of disruption is not stated in the table (possible link to ignition or deployment effects, unverified). [FLAG]


=====================================================================
FINDING 4 - Methanol shows a sharp regime change between 16% and 18% O2
=====================================================================
Metric: rho = Dext / D0 (rho near 1 = flame went out almost immediately; rho 0.4-0.5 = droplet burned down first).
Mean rho, extinction tests, 1 atm, N2 diluent: [CALC]
  O2     methanol (n)     heptane (n)
  0.12   0.69 (8)         -
  0.13   0.76 (5)         0.98 (3)
  0.14   0.66 (4)         1.00 (2)
  0.15   0.63 (5)         0.96 (3)
  0.16   -                0.90 (3)
  0.18   0.51 (4)         0.85 (2)
  0.21   0.49 (9)         0.74 (5)
  0.30   0.41 (2)         -
- Methanol: rho 0.8-1.0 at O2 <= 0.16, but 0.4-0.55 at O2 >= 0.18, for N2 and CO2 alike. [CALC/INF]
- This reproduces the two clusters seen in the Hicks methanol slides (13-16% O2 vs 18-21% O2).
- Heptane stays near rho = 1 up to about 18% O2 and only drops to 0.74-0.82 at 21%. It quenches
  radiatively over a wider O2 range than methanol.


=====================================================================
FINDING 5 - Radiatively quenched droplets burn for only ~2 seconds
=====================================================================
- Short-burn tests (rho >= 0.95) have median burn times at 1 atm of 1.6 s (heptane) and ~1.9 s (methanol). [CALC]
- Burn time by rho bin: rho 0.8-0.95 gives 4.5 s (heptane) and 7.8 s (methanol);
  rho <= 0.5 gives 10.2 s and 15.6 s. [CALC]
- Small droplets complete at low O2: heptane at 14-15% O2 burned to completion at D0 = 1.1-1.3 mm
  (3 tests), while a 4.6 mm droplet quenched after ~1.5 s (rho ~0.99). The flammability boundary for
  heptane at 15% O2 therefore lies between D0 = 1.3 and 2.5 mm. [CALC]


=====================================================================
FINDING 6 - Methanol extinction diameter vs initial diameter (1 atm, OLS, descriptive only)
=====================================================================
- O2 >= 0.18 (n = 30):  Dext = 0.43 * D0 + 0.06,   R^2 = 0.67  [CALC]
- O2 <= 0.16 (n = 34):  Dext = 1.26 * D0 - 1.39,   R^2 = 0.82  [CALC]
  (The intercept is not physical; it just describes a near-unity ratio over a limited D0 range.)


=====================================================================
FINDING 7 - Burning rate falls with droplet size and rises with oxygen
=====================================================================
In air, 1 atm, N2 diluent: [CALC]
- Heptane: Spearman rho = -0.63 (n = 15, p = 0.011), slope ~ -0.11 mm^2/s per mm of D0, K range 0.42-0.79.
- Methanol: Spearman rho = -0.69 (n = 12, p = 0.013), slope ~ -0.085 mm^2/s per mm of D0, K range 0.27-0.60.
- This independently supports the Liu et al. n-octane/n-decane finding with flight data for different fuels.

Mean K vs O2 in N2, 1 atm (mm^2/s): [CALC]
  Heptane:  0.25 (13% O2), 0.52 (14%), 0.53 (15%), 0.56 (21%), 0.74 (30%)
  Methanol: 0.36 (12%), 0.36 (13%), 0.34 (14%), 0.39 (15%), 0.43 (18%), 0.50 (21%), 0.67 (30%)


=====================================================================
FINDING 8 - n-Heptane in air at 1 atm matches the radiative-extinction picture
=====================================================================
- 17 heptane tests in air: completion 4, extinction 5, disruption 8. [CALC]
- Four of the five extinction tests went out near D^2 = 8-11 mm^2 after 8-10 s. This agrees with radiative
  extinction at D^2 ~ 10 mm^2 in the Nayagam slides and with the 6-7 s transition in the simulation. [INF]
- One test (N01H401, D0 = 3.32 mm) burned much longer and extinguished at 1.37 mm.
- Suggestive threshold (n = 5 only): D0 near 3.3 mm sits at the boundary between a single hot-flame stage
  and radiative extinction, consistent with simulation (3 mm single-stage, 4 mm and above two-stage). [INF]
- The table has no column for the cool-flame (second) stage; cool-flame data must come from raw data or video. [FLAG]


=====================================================================
FINDING 9 - Suppressant comparison: CO2 vs He vs N2
=====================================================================
Lowest O2 at which a flame was observed, 1 atm, and implied dilution phi = 1 - x_O2/0.209: [CALC]
  Diluent  Fuel      Lowest O2   phi    Cup-burner micro-g MEC (methane)
  N2       methanol  0.12        0.43   0.38
  N2       heptane   0.13        0.38   0.38
  CO2      methanol  0.14        0.33   0.19
  CO2      heptane   0.17        0.19   0.19
  He       methanol  0.13        0.38   0.31
  He       heptane   0.16        0.23   0.31
- Where the limit appears reached (heptane with N2 and CO2), droplet-implied dilution matches the cup-burner
  micro-g MEC (0.38 and 0.19) - a notable cross-check across very different geometries. A single matching
  pair is a cross-check, not proof of equivalence, and the cup-burner source is methane. [INF]
- Heptane: CO2 reaches the quench boundary at much lower dilution (0.19) than N2 (0.38), consistent with CO2
  being the most effective agent. [INF]
- He limits were not reached in the tested range, so no firm He ranking can be drawn.
- Methanol burning rate at matched O2: helium raises K (about +0.1 to +0.23 mm^2/s over N2 at 14-18% O2),
  CO2 is close to or slightly below N2. For heptane at 18-20% O2 the He-N2 difference is within noise.
  He bins hold only 1-3 tests, so this is indicative. [CALC/INF]
- Direction agrees with the simulation prediction that helium raises initial burning rate via conductive heat transfer.


=====================================================================
FINDING 10 - Pressure effect: lower pressure may raise the O2 needed
=====================================================================
- CO2-diluted heptane shows radiatively quenched tests up to O2 = 0.23 at 0.7 atm (9 tests) but only up to
  0.18 at 1 atm (4 tests). [CALC]
- Methanol short burns reach O2 = 0.18 at 0.7 atm (3 tests) vs 0.14 at 1 atm (1 test). [CALC]
- Suggestion: lower pressure raises the O2 needed to avoid radiative quench, i.e. LOI rises as pressure
  falls; low-pressure cabins may need more O2 reduction. Small n. [INF]
- 0.7 atm N2 tests exist only at O2 = 0.20, 0.30, 0.34, so no N2 limit is available at 0.7 atm.
- 2-3 atm: 9 tests, all at O2 0.21 / N2 0.09 / CO2 0.70; only 2 of 9 have an extinction diameter. [CALC]


=====================================================================
FINDING 11 - Cool-flame discovery and why it matters for fire safety (from the source papers)
=====================================================================
- After radiative extinction of a large n-heptane droplet, the droplet keeps regressing at a quasi-steady
  rate, ends at a finite "cool-flame extinction" diameter, and then a vapor cloud rapidly appears. Never seen
  before in 50+ years of droplet experiments. [SRC]
- Simulation (detailed kinetics) reproduces two-stage burning qualitatively: first-stage peak ~2000 K, second
  stage ~750 K. It over-predicts second-stage burning rate and under-predicts extinction diameter
  (~0.35 mm, a factor of ~3.5 below experiment). [SRC]
- Any enhanced heat loss (radiative CO2, conductive He, large droplet size) can push a droplet into the
  cool-flame branch. [INF]
- Implication: suppression by dilution may convert a hot flame into a cool one rather than extinguishing it,
  and the partially oxidized vapor can re-ignite (re-ignition has been shown with decane). Safety margins
  should be set by the end of the cool-flame stage, not just loss of visible flame. [INF]

FINDING 12 - Cross-source checks that held up
=====================================================================
1. Test 145 (id C01M201) equals the plot data box on Hicks slide 10: methanol, 763.5 mmHg, 20% O2 / 75% N2 /
   5% CO2, D0 = 3.33 mm, Dext = 1.61 mm, K = 0.45 mm^2/s. Slide 9 (75% CO2 / 5% N2) is the erroneous one. [CALC]
2. K units: the table spans 0.05-1.06 (typically 0.3-0.7), i.e. mm^2/s; "m^2/s" on slides is a unit typo. [CALC]
3. Hicks cluster plot is consistent with the methanol regime change in Finding 4.
4. Burning-rate decrease with D0 is reproduced in heptane and methanol flight data (Finding 7).
5. Cup-burner MEC agrees with droplet-implied dilution for heptane in N2 and CO2 (Finding 9).
6. Heptane radiative extinction at D^2 ~ 10 mm^2 matches 4 of 5 flight tests (Finding 8).
7. Simulated He effect on K matches the direction in the flight data.


=====================================================================
FINDING 13 - Data-quality flags in the flight table
=====================================================================
- 85 of 274 rows (31%) lack a usable visible-extinction diameter (blank in 49, zero in 8, plus 28 corrupt). D0 missing in 12 rows, K in 16. [CALC]
- File is Windows-1252, not UTF-8; 34 cells in 32 rows hold a corrupt byte instead of a number.
- Tests 70 and 73 share id 193F001 and the same timestamp - a duplicate record of one test.
- Test 114 (C11M201) has pressure = 0 mmHg and the same timestamp as test 118; both suspect.
- Test 129 has Dext (3.34) above D0 (3.33), within measurement error; test 130 has K = 0.05 with a 0.6 s burn,
  probably a fit artifact.
- Six rows (tests 69, 75, 76, 79, 80, 81) have composition sums off by more than 0.015 from 1.
- Burn times for tests 223 and 224 are approximate ("~6", "~9"); test 109 has no burn time.
- Dates mix MM/DD/YY and MM/DD/YYYY, and test numbers are not in chronological order (24 backward steps).
  Order by date and GMT, not test number.
- No explicit "no ignition" outcome exists, so every limit is an upper bound on LOI.
- The "CO" column almost certainly means CO2; the "Burning rate; mm" header must be mm^2/s. [INF]
- The meaning of the F and R suffixes in test ids, the cause of "Disruption", and each test's flow velocity are
  not in the table. Slow-flow tests cannot be identified. [FLAG]


=====================================================================
FINDING 14 - Unresolved source discrepancies
=====================================================================
- Three planning documents give different envelopes: Test Plan (0.5-1.5 atm quiescent, 0.7-1.0 suppressant,
  2-3 atm radiative, O2 0.10-0.40); EIRD v4 p.38 (0.5-3.0 atm, O2 0.1-0.4, suppressant 0-0.7); EIRD v4 p.29
  (O2 0.10-0.50). Always name the document a range came from. [FLAG]
- Suppressant lists differ: PSI text and Test Plan say CO2, He, SF6; a 2013 slide says N2, He, CO2, xenon;
  the flight table only has CO2 and He. [FLAG]
- Metadata archive is named PSI-133 but the study ID inside is PSI-69 (unexplained). [FLAG]
- The "Detailed results" report is linked to NTRS 20150023456 in the PSI list, not NASA/TP-2013-216046. [FLAG]
- Cup-burner CO2 vs He ranking conflicts with Honda & Ronney thin-solid flame-spread results; no single
  ranking holds across fuels and geometries. [SRC/INF]
- Values read from plots (temperatures, watts, thresholds) are approximate (about +/-10%). [FLAG]
